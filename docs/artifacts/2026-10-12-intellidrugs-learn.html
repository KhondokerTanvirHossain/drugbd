1Start here

IntelliDrugs links every medicine registered in Bangladesh to the substances inside it, to the codes other systems use, and to published interaction data. A pharmacist checks each record before anyone outside the team can see it.

What IntelliDrugs is

Start from a brand name a doctor writes, such as Napa. IntelliDrugs finds its DGDA registration, works out that it contains paracetamol 500 mg, and connects paracetamol to the same substance in RxNorm, ATC, DrugBank, SNOMED CT and DDInter 2.0. From there it can show which other substances it interacts with and how DDInter grades each interaction.

The website is drugdb.eliooo.org. Reviewers work on the batch pages. Doctors, pharmacists and partner systems read only what a pharmacist has approved.

Who it is for

Doctors and prescribers

Look up a brand, see what it contains, and check a list of medicines for DDInter interactions before you prescribe.

Pharmacists

Check a dispensing list, read DDInter's management text, and review records before they go public. Your approval is what publishes a record.

Partner companies

EMR, pharmacy and pharma systems call the API with DGDA products and get substances, codes and interaction alerts back.

What it is not

Not a prescribing system

It does not choose medicines, doses or durations. It is decision support: it shows what the sources say so a clinician can decide.

Not proof that a combination is safe

An empty alert list only means DDInter recorded nothing for those pairs. A substance DDInter does not cover is shown as no data, never as safe.

Not its own source of clinical text

Interaction and management text is DDInter 2.0's own wording. We shorten it on screen and link the full text. We never rewrite it.

Not public until a pharmacist approves

A loaded record stays hidden behind "Not yet reviewed by a pharmacist" until a pharmacist approves it. Engineer checks are recorded but do not publish.

2The model

Three levels carry every record: the brand product DGDA registered, the clinical drug it is, and the substances inside it. Codes and interactions hang off the substance, so one DDInter record serves every brand that contains it.

Brand product, clinical drug, substance

Brand product is a clinical drugMany brands, one clinical drug. Napa and any other paracetamol 500 mg tablet share BDC-000001.
Clinical drug contains substancesOne or more, each with its own strength. A combination tablet has two.
Substance is coded asMany codes per substance, each with how it was matched and a confidence from 0 to 1.
Substance is found in DDInter entriesZero or more, one per route. Domperidone has none. Chloramphenicol has three.
DDInter entry lists interactionsDrug–drug pairs, drug–disease and drug–food records, from DDInter 2.0.
Substance has coverageExactly one: full, or none. "None" is stored, never guessed from an empty list.
Brand product is reviewed in a batchEach batch record keeps stage results, raw evidence and every review decision.

3Our IDs and the code systems

External codes change, merge or go missing for medicines sold only in Bangladesh. So every substance and clinical drug gets our own permanent id, and the outside codes hang off it as crosswalks.

Why our own BDS and BDC ids

BDSBangladesh substance
-
000003Next number from a sequence

BDS-000003 is Warfarin.

BDCBangladesh clinical drug
-
000003Its own sequence

BDC-000003 is Warfarin 5 mg tablet.

  • Stable. A re-run looks up the existing id before minting a new one, so Warfarin stays BDS-000003 however often the pipeline runs.
  • Never reused. Neither sequence ever cycles, so a retired number can never point at a different medicine.
  • One substance per ICD-11 XM code. The database refuses a second substance for the same XM code, so Warfarin Sodium and Warfarin cannot split into two.
  • One clinical drug per signature. Dose form, route and sorted strengths make the key. Two brands of the same tablet share one BDC.
  • Covers what others miss. Domperidone has no DDInter entry, yet it still has BDS-000010 and six crosswalk codes.

Napa's substance, Acetaminophen BDS-000001, carries one code from each system. Method says how we found the code. Confidence runs from 0 to 1. Every pilot code was an exact match at 1.00.

4The process

Records enter in small batches. A pipeline runs four checks on each one and keeps every source response. People then compare the source with what we extracted, and only a pharmacist's approval makes a record public.

The whole flow

Batches of 10, then 50, then 100

A batch is a list of DGDA concept ids committed to the repository, picked in order of how many brands share each generic. Batches grow only when the last ones came back clean, by the size rule in the approved plan.

10records
Two batches in a row with at most 1 issue each and no systemic bug
50records
The same test again
100records

Drop back to 10 when a batch has more than 5% issues or any systemic bug. Stay at 10 until the cause is fixed and covered by a test.

We are at 10. Batch 0001-pilot holds 10 records and its status is in review.

5Why it works this way

Each rule below exists because the simpler choice would put a wrong or unchecked fact in front of a prescriber.

  • Why DGDA is the source of truth

    The DGDA register is the official list of what may be sold in Bangladesh: 39,196 products, each with a DAR number, mapped by the regulator to ICD-11 substance codes. The catalogue we had before was scraped, could not be shared with partners, and left many brands with no generic linked.

    Pilot: G Warfarin's ingredient comes from DGDA's own mapping, ICD-11XM86W0

  • Why interactions attach to substances

    Interactions happen between active ingredients, not brand names. Attaching them to the substance means one DDInter record covers every brand that contains it, and a combination product is checked ingredient by ingredient.

    Pilot: Amoxycillin Clavulanic Acid is checked as Amoxicillin BDS-000008 plus Clavulanic acid BDS-000007.

  • Why people review in small batches

    Extraction mistakes are cheap to fix when ten records are on the table and expensive after thousands. Automated extraction can make mistakes that look plausible, so a person compares the source with every extracted field.

    Pilot: 10 records, each with the raw DGDA row beside what we extracted.

  • Why no data never means safe

    Domperidone has no DDInter 2.0 entry (coverage: none). A missing entry says nothing about whether a combination is safe, so we say exactly that: No interaction data in DDInter for this substance. This does not mean it is safe to combine. Its pairs are marked "not checked".

    Pilot: No interaction data Domperidone

  • Why unclassified severity stands apart

    DDInter lists many pairs without a grade. Folding them into Minor would make hundreds of ungraded pairs look mild. They keep their own chip and their own count.

    Pilot: Warfarin has Minor 71Severity not classified by DDInter 298

  • Why only a pharmacist can publish

    Saying a brand contains a substance, and that the substance carries these interactions, is a clinical claim. Engineers check the extraction and their check is recorded, but only a pharmacist's approval reaches the public site and the API.

    Pilot: Not yet reviewed by a pharmacist on all 10 records, so none is public.

Drug–disease, drug–food and coverage

Drug–disease and drug–food

DDInter also links a substance to conditions and foods. In the pilot data these are counts only: Acetylsalicylic acid 9 drug–disease, Warfarin 2 drug–food. The condition and food names and their text are not in the pilot data.

Coverage

Every substance has a stored coverage value. full shows as neutral text, never green:

Interaction data found

none shows the full no-data statement, always expanded. A substance whose DDInter stage has not run yet is a different state: "Not checked yet: DDInter mapping has not run". It never gets the no-data sentence, because DDInter was never asked.

Safety fields you must display

These values are why the API exists. A partner screen that hides them can make missing data look like a clean result.

  • coverage: "none"
    noData[]
    No interaction dataShow in full: "No interaction data in DDInter for this substance. This does not mean it is safe to combine." Count its pairs as not checked.
  • severity: "unknown"
    Severity not classified by DDInterNever "low risk", never merged with Minor.
  • hidden: true
    reviewStatus other than "ok"
    notReviewed[]
    Not yet reviewed by a pharmacistNever "not found", never "no data".
  • routeAssumed: true
    Say the route was assumed: the product's own route had no DDInter entry, so the systemic entry was used.
  • interaction: null
    management: null
    DDInter has no text for this part. Leave it out; never print "None".
  • source: "DDInter 2.0"
    Attribute the text. If you shorten it, say "Full text from DDInter 2.0" and link to it.

Versioning and data releases

Now

  • Every path starts with /v1.
  • The database schema is locked at a reviewed version. Changes arrive as new reviewed migrations: a minor version for additions, a major version for anything that alters or removes structure.
  • Each response carries a timestamp and request id.

Planned

  • A monthly, read-only release of approved records, named by month, such as 2026.11.
  • Every response names its release, and each release is diffed against the last. No silent deletions.
  • Monthly DGDA and RxNorm refresh, quarterly DDInter re-crawl.
  • Planned endpoints: /v1/search, /v1/substances/{bdsId}/interactions, /v1/releases.

10FAQ

Short answers to the questions people ask first.

Questions

Why can't I see Napa on the public site?

No pharmacist has approved it yet. Every pilot record is Not yet reviewed by a pharmacist, so public pages and the public API show the name and that status, and nothing else.

No alerts came back. Is the combination safe?

Not necessarily. No alert means DDInter 2.0 records no interaction for those pairs. Check that no product is listed under not reviewed and no substance under no data; those pairs were not checked at all.

Why does Domperidone show no interaction data?

Domperidone has no DDInter 2.0 entry (coverage: none). We show: No interaction data in DDInter for this substance. This does not mean it is safe to combine.

Why does Napa say Acetaminophen, not Paracetamol?

DGDA's ICD-11 mapping and DDInter both name the substance Acetaminophen. Paracetamol is kept as a synonym, so searching either name finds Napa.

Can an engineer publish a record?

No. An engineer's "Looks correct" is recorded as Engineer checked and the record stays hidden. Only a pharmacist's approval publishes.

What if the record changes while I am reviewing it?

The page shows This record changed — reload to review the latest data and pauses decisions. A decision sent from the old page is refused, so you never approve data you did not see.

I pressed "Looks correct" by mistake.

Press U or Undo within 8 seconds. Nothing is saved or published until the window closes.

How do I become a reviewer?

Anyone can read approved records without signing in, and anyone can sign in with Google to report a problem. Reviewer roles are by invitation from the IntelliDrugs team.

Is my email shown to anyone?

No. Reviewer and reporter emails are stored for the team only. Public decision history shows the role, never the email.

Can my company use the API today?

Not yet directly. The API runs behind the website for now. Partner keys, metering and monthly releases are planned.
