One record, three kinds of work

The same pharmacist-approved record serves the prescriber at the desk, the pharmacist at the counter and the systems that move prescriptions around.

Doctors and prescribers

Prescribing and checking a patient's medicines

  • Find a brand by the name on the strip and see its substance and strength.
  • Check a patient's medicines against each other, Major first.
  • Read DDInter 2.0's own management text, truncated, never paraphrased.
Pharmacists

Dispensing, substitution and review

  • See the brands that share a clinical drug, for substitution.
  • Trace every record to its DGDA register entry and the checks it passed.
  • Invited pharmacists review records. Their approval publishes them.
Partner companies

EMR, pharmacy and pharma systems

  • Permanent BDS substance ids and BDC clinical drug ids.
  • Crosswalk codes to ICD-11, RxNorm, ATC, DrugBank and SNOMED CT.
  • An interaction check that returns pharmacist-approved data only.

How a record becomes trustworthy

Every product follows the same path. The last step before public is a person, and a re-run sends the record back to that person.

  1. 1
    DGDA register

    Trade name, company, dosage form and generic text, read from the DGDA register exactly as filed.

    Paracetamol 500 mg
  2. 2
    Extraction

    Substances and strengths become a clinical drug, with codes for each substance.

    Acetaminophen 500 mg tablet BDC-000001
  3. 3
    4 automated checks

    DGDA load, substances, clinical drug and DDInter. Each ends OK, Warning or Failed with the pipeline's own note.

    OKWarningFailed
  4. 4
    Pharmacist approval

    A pharmacist compares the register with the result. An engineer's check is recorded but never publishes.

    Not yet reviewed by a pharmacist
  5. 5
    Public

    Search, product pages, the checker and the partner API show the record.

    Approved by a pharmacist

For partners: the same records, by API

EMR, pharmacy and pharma systems get what the site shows: pharmacist-approved records, permanent ids and crosswalk codes, in one response envelope.

  • Check several products in one call. Alerts come sorted by severity, Major first.
  • No-data substances and unreviewed products are listed by name, never dropped.
  • Every response uses {success, data, message, errors, metadata}.

      What is marked Example, and why

      Everything else on this board is live pilot data. These are the places where the data doesn't exist yet.

      • Any approvalNo pilot record is approved. The landing hero, Napa's approved product page, the "paracetamol" results and the checker's framing show a future state, and the approval date 14/10/2026 is invented.
      • PeopleNusrat Jahan, Farhana Islam, their initials and their example.com addresses stand in for real readers and reviewers.
      • Other brands of a clinical drugNo other pilot brand shares BDC-000001, so the product page says so instead of listing brands.
      • Disease and food interactionsThe pilot carries counts only (Acetaminophen: 3 disease, 1 food), so the page shows counts and no detail.
      • Partner API fieldsThe endpoint and envelope come from the system design; the request and response field names are illustrative. Values are pilot data.
      • LicencesLicence lines for DDInter 2.0, ATC, DrugBank and SNOMED CT need confirming before launch.
      • Chloramphenicol routeThe pilot pairs use the systemic entry DDInter351 for an eye-drop product. DDInter352 (ophthalmic) pairs are not in the pilot data.
      • Pairs not in the pilot dataClavulanic acid with all 9 other substances, and Chloramphenicol with Metformin, have no pilot pair. They are counted apart from checked pairs and shown as "Not in the pilot's DDInter 2.0 pair list", with the caveat.
